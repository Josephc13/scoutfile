How it works

From "where is that file?" to the file, in three steps.

Scoutfile sits on top of the SharePoint sites your company already uses. It doesn't move your documents. It learns where they live, so anyone can find them by asking.

The problem

In most mid-size companies, files are spread across dozens of department sites with names that sound alike: "IT Help Center," "IT Resources," "Tech Support." An employee who needs the VPN guide or the safety-incident form has to guess which site owns it, then click through folders until they find it. Field and job-site staff, who work from phones, give up fastest and call the help desk instead. That wasted search time adds up to hours per person every month, and it slows down the people who can least afford to wait.

How Scout solves it

Pick a department, or search everything

Start in IT Help Center, Sales & Marketing, Operations, HR, or "All sites." Narrowing the scope first means Scout answers from the right team's documents, not a look-alike folder.

Ask in plain English

Type the question the way you'd ask a coworker: "How do I connect to the VPN from a job site?" or "How many PTO days do I get?" No file names or folder paths needed.

Get the answer and the source

Scout replies with the answer and the document it came from, so employees can trust it and open the original file when they need the details.

Who it's for

Scoutfile is built for companies with 100 to 5,000 employees, several departments, and a lot of people working away from a desk, such as construction, field services and multi-site operations. IT teams get fewer "where do I find…" tickets, HR answers fewer repeat policy questions, and employees get back to work faster.
